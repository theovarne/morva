# MILONO Organism Model

MILONO explores mutable form and persistent identity through computational and interface abstractions. It is not a biological simulation validated against living organisms.

**Active implementation:** optimized tardigrade console. **Retained alternative:** amoebic membrane renderer. **Proposed protocol:** private-substrate identity and body derivation. These three layers must not be conflated.

## Membrane

A soft boundary makes form change visible without declaring a new individual. In the retained [amoeba.js](dist/assets/amoeba.js) experiment, twelve local regions combine pressure, seeded noise and deformation. The active page does not load that script; it shows water-bear artwork and a neural-body canvas from [visuals.js](dist/assets/visuals.js).

## Pseudopods

Extensions, retractions and exploration express local response rather than rigid translation. The alternative renderer has bounded exploratory extensions and pointer-driven perturbations. These are visual field dynamics, not navigation, autonomous planning or live-site drag gestures.

## Body Field

The active runtime derives repeatable body-node and fingerprint values from public specimen identifiers. The 256-cell fingerprint is an inspectable fixture, not trained machine-learning weights. A proposed `W = HKDF(sk)` construction is documented in [Body Derivation](docs/body-derivation.md), not implemented by the browser.

## Cryptographic Core

The experimental nucleus symbolizes an identity-bearing origin; it stores no secret and provides no security. In the active console, identity is represented by fixture labels, roots and commitments shared by all panels. A change in stress or visual state does not change the selected fixture's root.

## Motility

Motility means visible activity in the interface, not measured locomotion. The alternative membrane renderer ties activity to lifecycle and stress. The active renderer instead varies neural-field contraction, node motion and signal intensity; no live motility-index measurement is claimed.

## Internal Flow

Cytoplasmic traces in the amoebic experiment visualize movement within a persistent boundary. The active neural canvas uses links and pulses. Neither is a fluid solver or a representation of real intracellular transport.

## Stress Response

The provider computes risk from five controls: latency, noise, compute, resource and pressure. For an active specimen, risk below 52 is ACTIVE; 52–69.999 is UNSTABLE; at least 70 enters TUN. TUN preserves fixtures and history while suspending ordinary cycles; risk below 52 permits explicit wake.

State-model buttons are demonstration overrides. DEAD is a simulated continuity failure with integrity zero, not detection of an actual compromised key. See [Lifecycle](docs/lifecycle.md).

## Runtime and History

[organism.js](dist/assets/organism.js) owns state, commands and persistence. [visuals.js](dist/assets/visuals.js) connects inspection panels. No remote inference service, private key or external execution is involved.

History retains at most 80 records and metabolism at most 72 samples. Refresh preserves current state through versioned browser storage. Selecting another fixture reinitializes its simulation; the active runtime does not preserve independent per-specimen histories. See [Specimens](docs/specimens.md).

## Research Requirements

A production cryptographic organism would need canonical encodings, a versioned derivation profile, signatures, verified ancestry, recovery and clone/fork semantics. These are open requirements, not completed features. The source and documentation expose this gap deliberately.
